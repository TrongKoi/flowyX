This is the agreement between you and the Flowy team about using the Flowy app on your device. We have tried to write it in ordinary English. Where legal wording is unavoidable, we explain it underneath.

# 1. What Flowy is

Flowy is a time and focus app designed for people with ADHD and for anyone who finds it hard to start a task. It includes a calendar, a focus timer, a journal, meeting minutes and the supporting tools around them.

Flowy is NOT a medical device, it does NOT diagnose anything, and it does NOT replace a doctor, therapist or psychologist. The suggestions inside the app — including the section that helps when a task feels impossible to start — are organisational tools, not medical advice. If you are struggling with your mental health, please talk to a professional.

# 2. Who may use it

You must be 16 or older to create an account on your own. Between 13 and 16, you need the consent of a parent or guardian. Under 13, this app is not for you.

If you are a parent or guardian and find that your child created an account without your consent, use the delete-account option in Settings — all of the data lives on that device and is erased at the same time.

# 3. Your account lives on your device

A Flowy account is NOT a server account. When you register, the app creates a profile inside the app's private storage on this device. We cannot see that account, we cannot reset your password for you, and we cannot move your data to another device.

What follows from that, before you rely on it:

- The Gmail address you enter is only a username. We do not send mail to it, and we have no way to.
- The 12-character recovery code shown at registration is the only way back into your account if you forget your password. Write it down somewhere safe.
- Losing both the password and the recovery code means losing the data. The app lets you wipe everything and start again, but nobody can reopen the old data — including us.
- Uninstalling the app, clearing app data in Android Settings, or a factory reset all erase everything permanently.

You are responsible for keeping your password and recovery code. If someone can unlock your phone, they can open Flowy.

When you import an .ics file or a calendar from another service, the parsing happens entirely on your device. You are responsible for having the right to use that calendar data. If an imported event clashes with an existing plan, the app asks you before changing anything.

You can turn the connection off at any time in Settings, and the data read so far is deleted at the same moment. Details are in the corresponding section of the Privacy Policy.
